# S02 "From Quartz to a Chip": research note + shot plan (2026-11-02)

## Verified facts (sources; the researcher read text pages only, no videos)
- Quartz = SiO2 (textbook). Carbothermic reduction in an arc furnace (>1900 °C) → metallurgical-grade Si, ~98 % (mks.com/n/silicon-wafer-production).
- Siemens route: Si + HCl (~300 °C) → trichlorosilane, distilled, decomposed in hydrogen on ~1100 °C silicon rods → polysilicon with ppb impurities (pv-manufacturing.org; Fraunhofer ISE). Say "parts-per-billion", not one exact "N".
- Czochralski: polysilicon melted in a quartz crucible, seed dipped and pulled slowly while rotating; thin neck first to remove dislocations; up to 300 mm diameter. Say "above 1400 °C", pull "a few cm per hour"; no ingot length stated.
- Slicing: many parallel wires/diamond wire cut hundreds of wafers at once; lapping, chemical etch, CMP leave one mirror side. 300 mm wafer ≈ 775 µm thick, with a notch (not a flat).
- Transistor counts are per chip (A17 Pro ≈ 19 billion; Intel: "billions of tiny switches in an area no larger than a fingernail").
- MOSFET: gate above threshold forms a channel source→drain (ON), below it no channel (OFF). Standard physics, unsourced here; keep it at that level.
## Honesty rules for the visuals
Grey metallic ingot (the melt glows, the ingot does not); neck + shoulder; a BANK of wires, not one blade; wafers are thin; mirror on the front only; zoom on ONE die, transistors labelled illustrative. Not found: practitioner shot techniques (only descriptions), melt-temperature page, ingot length, MOSFET source. Technique here is our own design.
