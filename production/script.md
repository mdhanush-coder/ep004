### S02 — From Quartz to a Chip (37.41 s)
[fascinated] Our journey begins with silicon, found in materials such as quartz.
But ordinary sand cannot simply become a processor.
Silicon must be refined to extraordinary purity, grown into a single crystal, sliced into thin wafers, and polished.
Then microscopic structures are created on the wafer.
[confident, with emphasis] At their heart are transistors: tiny electrical switches.
One switch is simple. Billions working together form the foundation of modern computing.
[awed, building wonder] Follow one tiny section of this wafer, and the scale becomes almost unbelievable.

### S03 — The Precision Problem (58.36 s)
[serious, intrigued] Why can't every country make advanced chips?
[confident] Because the process demands extraordinary precision, repeated layer after layer.
In photolithography, a light-sensitive coating is applied to the wafer.
Light transfers a carefully designed pattern.
The material is developed, selected areas are etched, and new layers are added.
The wafer passes through many specialised stages, all requiring precise alignment and control.
[tense, low voice] Now imagine a microscopic particle landing on the surface.
At this scale, contamination can damage tiny features and reduce the number of usable chips.
[firm, confident] That is why fabrication plants need controlled cleanrooms, specialised machines, pure chemicals and gases, and deep technical expertise.
Even after fabrication, the chip must be cut, packaged, tested and verified.

### S04 — Why India Needs the Capability (47.48 s)
[engaged, purposeful] Now bring this challenge back to India.
Chips power far more than phones: cars, medical equipment, communication networks, defence systems, satellites, data centres, and AI.
[serious] Yet manufacturing is concentrated in a relatively small number of global industrial hubs.
A delayed shipment can affect industries far away.
For a country building its technological future, that dependence creates a strategic vulnerability.
[confident] India already has semiconductor design capabilities.
The challenge is to strengthen the wider ecosystem: manufacturing, equipment, materials, packaging, testing, research, and talent.
[determined] The goal is not simply to assemble electronics. It is to create more of the technology behind them.
