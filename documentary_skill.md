# XPLAINEDUNSEEN — EPISODE PRODUCTION BRIEF

# WHY INDIA IS BUILDING ITS OWN COMPUTER CHIPS

**Format:** 5-minute cinematic engineering documentary  
**Audience:** Viewers across India  
**Production basis:** The supplied *Animated Semiconductor Documentary* PDF only  
**Primary engines:** Blender + Unreal Engine  
**Creative objective:** Make the audience curious, visually engaged and able to understand the semiconductor journey without feeling that they are watching a slow lecture.

---

## 1. The non-negotiable creative direction

This film is **not a factory tour, a school lesson or a patriotic montage**. It is a five-minute journey of one tiny piece of silicon. The audience follows it from an ordinary raw material, through transformation and microscopic manufacturing, into the larger question of India's technological future.

The emotional progression is:

**Curiosity → wonder → difficulty → national relevance → real progress → an open question about the future.**

The semiconductor manufacturing process is the visual hero. India's journey is the emotional hero.

### The central story question

India can build software, smartphones, satellites, cars and AI systems. But what happens when the tiny components powering those systems depend on manufacturing concentrated elsewhere?

### The storytelling device

Follow **one imaginary silicon chip** throughout the film. It is the connecting character:

1. It begins as raw silicon-bearing material.
2. It becomes a purified crystal and a wafer.
3. It passes through microscopic circuit manufacturing.
4. It reaches packaging and testing.
5. Its journey leads to the question of what India must master to build a complete semiconductor ecosystem.

Do not make the audience feel that each chapter is a disconnected explainer. Use visual match cuts and camera transitions to carry the same chip from one stage to the next.

## 3. Retention-first editing rules — prevent slow pacing

Cinematic does **not** mean slow. The film should feel premium, controlled and fast-moving. Camera movement may be smooth, but the viewer must keep discovering something new.

### Shot rhythm

- Use a typical shot length of **2–5 seconds** during active explanation and montage sections.
- Allow a hero shot to run **5–7 seconds** only when a meaningful reveal is happening.
- Avoid static shots, empty establishing shots or unchanging camera moves that run beyond **7 seconds**.
- Do not hold on a finished object after the viewer has already understood it. Reveal it, communicate its purpose, then move on.
- Every **3–5 seconds**, give the viewer a new visual piece of information: a new scale, angle, object, process step, comparison, label or consequence.
- Every **15–20 seconds**, create a clear pattern change: cut from macro to wide, move from animation to real-world context, change the visual scale, introduce a process complication or reveal a new location.
- Use short, purposeful pauses for suspense. Do not turn pauses into dead air.

These are editing targets for this production, not claims about the semiconductor process.

### No slow-motion filler

- Do not use slow motion as a substitute for storytelling.
- Do not turn a generated still image into a long, slow zoom just to fill runtime. Add purposeful parallax, depth separation, animated details, a graphic reveal or a quick cut — otherwise use it as a brief insert.
- Do not stretch camera pushes, wafer rotations, factory fly-throughs or particle animations to fill time.
- Do not let a camera drift towards an object for several seconds before anything happens.
- Do not repeat the same orbit, push-in or floating-particle shot with different assets.
- Do not keep showing a chip glowing without revealing new information.
- Do not use long fades between every chapter. Prefer motivated match cuts, wipes created by objects crossing frame, hard cuts on sound, or camera moves that connect spaces.

### Every shot must earn its place

Every shot must do at least one of these jobs:

1. **Explain** a process or relationship.
2. **Reveal** a new scale, detail or location.
3. **Create curiosity** or raise a question.
4. **Increase tension** by showing a constraint, risk or failure point.
5. **Advance the story** towards India's semiconductor ambitions.

If a shot does none of these, remove it — even if it is visually impressive.

### Keep the viewer oriented

Use only short, useful on-screen labels. Examples from the story: `SILICON`, `WAFER`, `TRANSISTOR`, `PHOTOLITHOGRAPHY`, `FABRICATION`, `ASSEMBLY & TESTING`, `DHOLERA`, `SANAND`. Avoid paragraph-sized text, technical definition cards and repeated title animations. Let the image explain; use text only to clarify.

## 4. Detailed scene direction and narration

The narration below is the continuous story spine. Visual directions are designed to keep the imagery changing while preserving continuity. Do not add long pauses between paragraphs. Keep narration curious, intelligent and confident — never like a classroom lecture or political speech.

### SCENE 01 — THE INVISIBLE BRAIN

**00:00–00:35 | Engine: Unreal Engine | Purpose: Hook**

**Visual sequence**

- 00:00 — Begin in darkness. A smartphone vibrates on a table; its screen lights the frame.
- 00:03 — Fingerprint unlock. Cut immediately to a UPI payment confirmation.
- 00:07 — A train ticket appears on a phone. Match cut to a car dashboard lighting up.
- 00:12 — Satellite over Earth. Quick return to the smartphone.
- 00:16 — Freeze the activity. Sound drops out for a brief beat.
- 00:18 — Camera pushes through the phone screen, passes the electronic layers and reaches the processor.
- 00:27 — Processor fills the frame. Circuit traces illuminate and the camera dives towards the silicon.
- 00:33 — Use the chip's surface as the transition into the next scene.

**Narration**

> Every day, you unlock a phone, send a UPI payment, book a train ticket or ask artificial intelligence a question. A car starts. A satellite carries information across the planet. Different experiences, one hidden foundation: computer chips. Inside them, billions of microscopic switches process information. India builds software used around the world. So why is building the chips themselves such a difficult challenge?

**Sound**

Start with a low cinematic pulse. Give each familiar action a crisp, recognisable sound. Build a subtle digital rhythm as the camera enters the phone. Cut the sound briefly before the central question. Do not leave a long silent gap.

**Retention checkpoint**

By 00:35, viewers should understand that a hidden chip connects familiar experiences — and should want to know how it is made.

---

### SCENE 02 — FROM QUARTZ TO A CHIP

**00:35–01:25 | Engine: Blender | Purpose: Visual wonder and simple science**

**Visual sequence**

- Start with the same chip journey moving backwards into its origin: reveal quartz or silicon-bearing raw material.
- Transform the material into highly purified silicon. Make the transformation visible rather than presenting a static model.
- Show a single-crystal ingot forming; cut to a thin wafer being sliced from it.
- Show the wafer being polished. Use moving reflections to communicate the smooth surface.
- Travel across the wafer, select one small region and dive into it.
- Reveal a transistor as a simple electrical switch. Show two clear states rather than a complicated technical diagram.
- Pull back to a dense, repeating transistor array. Communicate scale through repetition; do not attempt to display billions of individual models.
- End with the array resolving into the chip that the audience has been following.

**Narration**

> Our journey begins with silicon, found in materials such as quartz. But ordinary sand cannot simply become a processor. Silicon must be refined to extraordinary purity, grown into a single crystal, sliced into thin wafers and polished. Then microscopic structures are created on the wafer. At their heart are transistors: tiny electrical switches. One switch is simple. Billions working together form the foundation of modern computing. Follow one tiny section of this wafer, and the scale becomes almost unbelievable.

**Sound**

Use crystalline textures for the material transformation, restrained mechanical sounds for slicing and polishing, and precise electrical pulses when the transistor switches states. Give the transistor-array reveal a strong impact.

**Retention checkpoint**

By 01:25, viewers should be able to explain the basic journey: purified silicon → wafer → microscopic transistors → computing.

---

### SCENE 03 — THE PRECISION PROBLEM

**01:25–02:25 | Engines: Blender + Unreal Engine | Purpose: Tension and scale**

**Visual sequence**

- The wafer enters a precision machine. Keep it moving through the process; avoid a static machine beauty shot.
- Show the photolithography sequence in clearly readable steps: light-sensitive coating, patterned light exposure, development, etching and repeated layer work.
- Connect each stage with a continuous wafer movement or a match cut so it feels like one manufacturing journey.
- Cut to a large, immersive cleanroom environment. Engineers and equipment establish the industrial scale.
- Introduce a particle above the wafer. Freeze briefly, magnify the particle and compare its scale with a human hair and the microscopic structures.
- Show the particle threatening a tiny wafer feature. Communicate that contamination can damage microscopic features and reduce yield; do not imply that one particle necessarily destroys an entire factory.
- Resume the wafer journey through more precision stages.
- End on the packaged-and-tested destination as a visual hand-off to the next chapter.

**Narration**

> Why can't every country make advanced chips? Because the process demands extraordinary precision, repeated layer after layer. In photolithography, a light-sensitive coating is applied to the wafer. Light transfers a carefully designed pattern. The material is developed, selected areas are etched, and new layers are added. The wafer passes through many specialised stages, all requiring precise alignment and control. Now imagine a microscopic particle landing on the surface. At this scale, contamination can damage tiny features and reduce the number of usable chips. That is why fabrication plants need controlled cleanrooms, specialised machines, pure chemicals and gases, and deep technical expertise. Even after fabrication, the chip must be cut, packaged, tested and verified.

**Sound**

Layer industrial machinery, precise mechanical clicks and a high-pitched lithography texture. Cut to near-silence at the particle reveal, then bring the industrial sound back as the process continues. Keep the pause short and intentional.

**Retention checkpoint**

By 02:25, viewers should feel that chipmaking is difficult because of precision, repeated layers, contamination control, specialised equipment and expertise — not because the process is mysterious magic.

---

### SCENE 04 — WHY INDIA NEEDS THE CAPABILITY

**02:25–03:35 | Engine: Unreal Engine | Purpose: Make the science matter to India**

**Visual sequence**

- Leave the cleanroom through a match cut into a digital map of India.
- Move through a connected series of recognisable systems: a car, medical equipment, communication towers, a satellite, data centres and AI infrastructure.
- Keep the map as the visual anchor while locations and systems light up. Avoid turning this into a generic city montage.
- Show the global semiconductor supply chain as connected stages in different locations: design, fabrication, packaging and testing.
- Interrupt the flow with one delayed shipment. Show the effect moving downstream to other industries.
- Return to India and show the different capabilities that need to connect into an ecosystem.

**Narration**

> Now bring this challenge back to India. Chips power far more than phones: cars, medical equipment, communication networks, defence systems, satellites, data centres and AI. Yet manufacturing is concentrated in a relatively small number of global industrial hubs. A delayed shipment can affect industries far away. For a country building its technological future, that dependence creates a strategic vulnerability. India already has semiconductor design capabilities. The challenge is to strengthen the wider ecosystem: manufacturing, equipment, materials, packaging, testing, research and talent. The goal is not simply to assemble electronics. It is to create more of the technology behind them.

**Sound**

Transition from cleanroom ambience to a restrained Indian city and infrastructure soundscape. Build the score as the supply-chain connections appear. Keep the tone ambitious, not triumphalist or conventionally patriotic.

**Retention checkpoint**

By 03:35, viewers should understand why chipmaking capability matters to India's resilience and technological development. Do not imply that India cannot design chips; the challenge described here is to strengthen the wider ecosystem.

---

### SCENE 05 — INDIA'S SILICON BET

**03:35–04:35 | Engines: Unreal Engine + Blender | Purpose: Specific progress, not vague ambition**

**Visual sequence**

- Begin with a dark map of India. Bring Gujarat into focus.
- Move to a clearly labelled conceptual architectural reconstruction of the Tata Electronics semiconductor fabrication facility at Dholera. Identify it as **under development**; do not portray it as an operating fab.
- Make a decisive transition to Sanand. Show a separate assembly-and-testing environment for Micron and a separate facility reference for CG Semi.
- Use clear process labels: `FABRICATION` for wafer circuit creation; `ASSEMBLY & TESTING` for the later production stages.
- Show wafer handling, inspection, packaging and testing as a sequence of active operations.
- Bring in the approved-unit and investment figures as clean, brief data overlays. Keep the images moving behind or around the data; do not turn the scene into a static statistics slide.
- End by pulling back from a tested chip to a connected ecosystem of facilities, people, equipment, materials and research.

**Narration**

> India's ambitions are moving from announcements towards facilities and production. Gujarat is a major focus. Tata Electronics is developing a semiconductor fabrication facility at Dholera; the first fab is scheduled for commissioning in 2028. At Sanand, Micron's facility performs assembly and testing, not wafer fabrication. CG Semi's Sanand facility began commercial production in July 2026. Fabrication creates circuits on wafers; assembly and testing turn them into finished, verified products. The source records twelve approved units under Semicon India 1.0, and three in commercial production: Micron, Kaynes and CG Semi. It records more than ₹1.64 lakh crore in cumulative proposed investment. In July 2026, Semicon 2.0 was approved with a proposed outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore. The ambition is bigger than one factory: build the ecosystem behind the chip.

**On-screen data — use short, readable overlays**

- `12 APPROVED MANUFACTURING UNITS` — Semicon India 1.0.
- `3 IN COMMERCIAL PRODUCTION` — Micron, Kaynes and CG Semi.
- `₹1.64 LAKH CRORE+` — cumulative proposed investment.
- `₹1,27,500 CRORE` — proposed Semicon 2.0 outlay, approved in July 2026.

**Sound**

Shift from industrial tension into a confident, uplifting orchestral progression. Let the music build with the sequence of facilities and data, but keep narration clear and forward-moving.

**Retention checkpoint**

By 04:35, viewers should know that India has distinct projects and production stages underway, and should understand the difference between a fabrication facility and an assembly-and-testing facility.

### SCENE 06 — THE FUTURE IS BUILT LAYER BY LAYER

**04:35–05:00 | Engine: Unreal Engine | Purpose: Emotional payoff**

**Visual sequence**

- A young Indian engineer stands in a cleanroom. Keep the shot active with a purposeful camera move, not a long portrait hold.
- A wafer catches the light. Move past the engineer and dive through the wafer into the transistor landscape.
- The microscopic structures gradually form the outline of India.
- Resolve the image into one finished processor on a dark, controlled background.
- Display the end card within the final seconds: `INDIA'S SILICON FUTURE IS BEING BUILT. LAYER BY LAYER.` Then `XPLAINEDUNSEEN — TECHNOLOGY IS MORE INTERESTING THAN IT LOOKS.`

**Narration**

> But a semiconductor industry is not built overnight. India needs deeper expertise in advanced manufacturing, equipment, materials, research and global supply chains. The real test is not how many factories are announced. It is what the country can master, manufacture reliably and create for itself. Follow the silicon again: raw material, wafer, microscopic circuits, finished chip. The next technological revolution will not be powered by software alone. It will also be built on silicon. Can India build an entire future around it?

**Sound**

Bring the score to its emotional peak beneath the final question. Let the final processor reveal land cleanly. Do not append a long logo animation or an unnecessary extra montage.

**Retention checkpoint**

At 05:00, end on the question. Leave the audience with ambition and curiosity, not a long explanatory recap.

## 6. Generative AI image workflow — use it as another production tool

Generative AI image creation is part of this production pipeline. Use it to increase visual variety, create cinematic supporting imagery and solve shots that would otherwise require disproportionate modelling time. It must support the story, not become a shortcut that weakens technical accuracy.

### Where generative AI adds value

- **Opening chapter:** Create cinematic supporting images for abstract technology moments, satellite views, dramatic macro chip imagery and environmental cutaways. Use real or accurately recreated UPI and device interfaces when the details matter; do not rely on AI-generated text or interface labels.
- **Silicon origin:** Generate visual concept references for raw quartz, crystal growth, polished silicon and macro material studies. Use Blender for the actual transformation, wafer slicing and transistor animation so the science remains controllable and clear.
- **Precision and cleanroom:** Generate mood boards and supporting cinematic cleanroom or machine images. Use Blender for the wafer, lithography steps, particle comparison and any technical close-up that must show a specific process accurately.
- **India and the supply chain:** Generate supplementary atmospheric imagery for broad industrial scale or abstract global connections. Build the India map, locations, supply-chain links, data overlays and cause-and-effect sequence in Unreal Engine or motion graphics so they remain readable and correct.
- **Dholera and Sanand:** Do **not** use generative AI to invent documentary-looking images of the actual facilities. Use verified reference photography and grounded reconstructions. AI-generated generic factory imagery may only be used as a clearly labelled conceptual visualisation, never as proof of a real facility or its current operating state.
- **Closing chapter:** Generate visual concepts for the final engineer-and-wafer composition, lighting references or background plates. Keep the final wafer, microscopic structures and India reveal under direct 3D or compositing control.

### The AI image production process

1. **Start with the shot's job.** Write one sentence explaining what the image must communicate, where it appears in the story and what the viewer should notice first. Do not generate images just to decorate empty timeline space.
2. **Create a small option set.** Generate a few compositions for the same shot, compare them side by side and select the clearest one. Prefer a readable silhouette and one dominant subject over a crowded image.
3. **Lock a visual prompt guide.** Keep the same visual language across the episode: photorealistic engineering documentary, graphite and metallic surfaces, restrained cyan lighting, realistic materials, controlled contrast and cinematic 16:9 framing. Use restrained saffron accents only in the India chapter.
4. **Preserve continuity.** Reuse approved reference images, subject descriptions, lighting language and composition rules for recurring subjects. The silicon chip should remain recognisably the same visual thread. If a recurring engineer appears in more than one generated image, use a consistent reference image and clothing description; reject outputs where identity or wardrobe changes noticeably.
5. **Generate clean frames.** Ask for no text, no captions, no logos, no watermarks and no fake brand marks. Add all labels, figures, maps and typography later in the edit or motion-graphics system. Leave enough negative space for overlays when a shot needs them.
6. **Prepare images for motion.** Where useful, separate foreground, subject and background into depth layers, then create restrained 2.5D parallax or camera movement in Blender, Unreal Engine or the available compositing tools. Add small motivated movement — lights, reflections, machinery indicators or atmosphere — only when it supports the shot.
7. **Treat every generated image as a draft until checked.** Inspect geometry, hands, machinery, wafer patterns, chip details, reflections, text artefacts, repeated objects and physical plausibility. Regenerate or replace any image that looks broken, misleading or inconsistent with adjacent shots.
8. **Keep the edit moving.** Use generated stills as short inserts, animated plates or transitions. A still image must not become a long, unchanging shot. If it needs more than a brief hold, add meaningful motion or rebuild the scene in 3D.

### What generative AI must not replace

- Do not use a generated still as the only explanation of how silicon is refined, how a wafer is processed, how photolithography works or how a transistor switches. These need clear, controllable animation.
- Do not use generated images to fabricate real factory exteriors, company logos, government project signage, semiconductor statistics, maps or claims about operational status.
- Do not trust generated text, technical labels, chip layouts, process diagrams or facility signage. Create these accurately in the relevant 3D, graphics or editing tools.
- Do not mix visibly different AI art styles, inconsistent lighting or unrelated colour palettes in the same sequence.
- Do not use an attractive AI image if it contradicts the narration or gives viewers the wrong impression.

**Decision rule:** Use generative AI when the shot benefits from a strong designed image; use Blender when geometry, materials or scientific process must be exact; use Unreal Engine when an explorable environment, real-time cinematic camera or large connected space is needed. Combine them when that produces a better shot. The production tool must serve the shot — never force the shot to serve the tool.

## 8. Factual guardrails from the supplied source

The following distinctions are essential to the story and must remain clear in narration, labels and visuals:

1. **Silicon:** Quartz contains silicon dioxide; high-purity silicon requires extensive refining.
2. **Transistors:** Tiny electronic switches that form the basis of digital logic.
3. **Photolithography:** Transfers patterns onto a light-sensitive coating on the wafer.
4. **Fabrication:** Involves repeated processes, including deposition, lithography, etching and doping.
5. **Packaging:** Connects, protects and prepares the manufactured semiconductor for use.
6. **India's capabilities:** Design, fabrication, assembly, testing and packaging are distinct capabilities. Do not imply that India has no chip-design capability.
7. **Tata Electronics at Dholera:** The fabrication facility is under development; the source states that the first fab is scheduled for commissioning in 2028.
8. **Micron at Sanand:** The facility performs assembly and testing, not wafer fabrication.
9. **CG Semi at Sanand:** The source states that its OSAT facility began commercial production in July 2026.
10. **Source milestone figures dated 2 November 2026:** 12 approved manufacturing units under Semicon India 1.0; 3 units in commercial production (Micron, Kaynes and CG Semi); ₹1.64 lakh crore+ cumulative proposed investment; and a proposed Semicon 2.0 outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore approved in July 2026.

Do not turn proposed investment into money already spent. Do not describe a facility under development as operational. Do not confuse wafer fabrication with assembly and testing. Do not add unsupported claims, numbers, timelines or facility capabilities to the film.
